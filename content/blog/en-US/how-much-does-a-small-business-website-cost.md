## What drives the price of a website

Two quotes for "a website" can describe very different things. These are the factors that matter most:

- **Type of site.** A single page is less work than a multi-page site, or an online store with products, payments and orders.
- **Custom design or template.** A generic template is cheaper to set up, but it makes your business look like many others. A design built around your brand stands out.
- **Content.** Who writes the copy and handles the photos? Creating everything from scratch adds work.
- **Features.** Booking, customer accounts and integrations with other tools add development time.
- **SEO and speed.** A fast site that's well structured for Google takes more care to build, but it's what brings in customers without paying for ads.

## What's usually left out of the quote

When you compare proposals, check whether these are included:

1. **Domain name** (for example, `yourbusiness.com`) — renewed every year.
2. **Hosting** — the server your site lives on.
3. **Business email** — `hello@yourbusiness.com` instead of a generic address.
4. **Maintenance** — updates, content changes and ongoing SEO work.

A very low quote that leaves all of this out can end up costing more.

## Questions to ask before you hire

- Is the design made for my brand, or is it an adapted template?
- Is the site fast on mobile?
- Is it set up to show up on Google (titles, descriptions, structured data)?
- Who owns the domain and the site when the project is done?
- What does it cost to change something after launch?

## FAQ

**Is a $300 website enough for a small business?**
To present a service and capture leads, a well-built landing page is often enough. If you need several pages or want to sell online, a business website or an online store makes more sense.

**How long does it take to build a website?**
It depends on the project and who builds it. At RUPE, your site is live 2 days after your request.

**Do I need maintenance?**
It's not required. But a site without updates or SEO work tends to lose ground on Google over time.
